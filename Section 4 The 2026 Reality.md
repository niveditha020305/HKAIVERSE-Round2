# 4. The 2026 Reality

The R22 CSE curriculum provides a strong foundation in programming, algorithms, systems, databases, software engineering, artificial intelligence and cybersecurity. However, the computing field has changed considerably since the curriculum was designed. The following changes are particularly relevant when comparing the R22 curriculum with the technology landscape of 2026.

## 4.1 Generative AI and Large Language Models

Generative AI and Large Language Models (LLMs) have become an important part of modern software and AI development. NIST published a dedicated Generative AI Profile in 2024 to address risks and practices associated with generative-AI systems, including their design, development, use and evaluation. This indicates that generative AI has developed into a distinct area requiring dedicated technical and governance practices. The existing foundations of AI and ML remain important, but students should also be exposed to LLMs, generative models, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), model evaluation and responsible AI practices.

**Affected courses:** CS603PC – Artificial Intelligence; CS601PC – Machine Learning

**Source:** NIST, *Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile*, 2024. Accessed September 2026.

## 4.2 AI Risk Management and Responsible AI

AI development has increasingly moved beyond simply building models toward managing their reliability, security, privacy and societal risks. NIST's Generative AI Profile identifies risks associated with generative-AI systems and provides actions for managing these risks throughout the AI lifecycle. Modern AI education therefore needs to include not only algorithms but also evaluation, reliability, privacy, security, bias and responsible deployment.

**Affected courses:** CS603PC – Artificial Intelligence; CS601PC – Machine Learning; CS405PC – Software Engineering

**Source:** NIST, *Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile*. Accessed September 2026.

## 4.3 AI-Assisted Software Development

AI-assisted programming has become a significant part of the modern developer workflow. GitHub's 2025 Octoverse reported major changes in software development driven by AI and AI agents. The 2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey also reported widespread use of AI tools by developers, while showing concerns about the reliability of AI-generated output. This means that programmers increasingly need to know how to use AI tools while still being able to review, debug, test and validate generated code.

**Affected courses:** CS103ES – Programming for Problem Solving; CS305PC – Object Oriented Programming through Java; CS405PC – Software Engineering

**Sources:** GitHub Octoverse 2025; Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025. Accessed September 2026.

## 4.4 Cloud-Native Development and Kubernetes

Cloud-native development has become an important foundation for modern software infrastructure. The CNCF 2026 Annual Cloud Native Survey reported widespread production use of Kubernetes and its increasing use for AI workloads. This represents a shift from traditional server-based deployment toward containerized, automated and scalable infrastructure. The R22 DevOps curriculum should therefore be updated with technologies such as Kubernetes, CI/CD, cloud deployment, GitOps and modern monitoring.

**Affected courses:** CS503PC – DevOps; CS505PC – DevOps Lab; CS601PC – Machine Learning

**Source:** CNCF, *Annual Cloud Native Survey: The Infrastructure of AI's Future*, 2026. Accessed September 2026.

## 4.5 Post-Quantum Cryptography

Cryptography is changing because future quantum computers could threaten several widely used public-key cryptographic systems. In August 2024, NIST finalized three Post-Quantum Cryptography standards: FIPS 203, FIPS 204 and FIPS 205. NIST has also emphasized the need for organizations to plan migration toward quantum-resistant cryptography. Therefore, modern cybersecurity education should introduce students to the basic concepts and standards of post-quantum cryptography while retaining classical cryptographic foundations.

**Affected courses:** CS701PC – Cryptography and Network Security; CS511PE – Quantum Computing

**Source:** NIST, *Post-Quantum Cryptography Standards*, 2024. Accessed September 2026.

## 4.6 Security of LLM and Generative-AI Applications

The growth of generative AI has introduced security problems that are different from many traditional software and network attacks. OWASP's 2025 Top 10 for LLM Applications identifies risks such as prompt injection, sensitive information disclosure, supply-chain vulnerabilities, data and model poisoning, and improper output handling. This means that modern security education should also consider the security of AI-based applications and the interaction between models, users and external data.

**Affected courses:** CS701PC – Cryptography and Network Security; CS603PC – Artificial Intelligence; CS405PC – Software Engineering

**Source:** OWASP, *Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025*. Accessed September 2026.

## 4.7 Databases Beyond Traditional Relational Systems

Modern AI applications increasingly use additional data-storage and retrieval mechanisms alongside traditional relational databases. Embeddings, vector search and retrieval-augmented generation have created new requirements for storing and retrieving high-dimensional data. This affects the traditional DBMS curriculum, which should retain relational database concepts while introducing students to NoSQL concepts, vector databases, embeddings and their use in modern AI applications.

**Affected courses:** CS404PC – Database Management Systems; CS407PC – Database Management Systems Lab; CS603PC – Artificial Intelligence

**Source:** OWASP, *Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025*. Accessed September 2026.

## 4.8 AI Deployment and Production Infrastructure

AI is no longer limited to model development and experimentation. Organizations increasingly need to deploy, monitor and scale AI systems in production. The CNCF 2026 survey highlights the growing use of cloud-native infrastructure for AI workloads, including model inference. This creates a stronger connection between machine learning and software infrastructure. Students should therefore understand the basic lifecycle of taking a trained model from development to deployment, monitoring and maintenance.

**Affected courses:** CS601PC – Machine Learning; CS503PC – DevOps; CS502PC – Computer Networks

**Source:** CNCF, *Annual Cloud Native Survey: The Infrastructure of AI's Future*, 2026. Accessed September 2026.

## Overall Finding

The major issue identified is not that the R22 curriculum lacks fundamental Computer Science knowledge. Subjects such as algorithms, operating systems, databases, networks, AI, machine learning and cybersecurity continue to provide important foundations. The larger issue is that several courses reflect a technology environment that existed before the rapid growth of generative AI, cloud-native infrastructure, AI-assisted development and standardized post-quantum cryptography.

Therefore, the evidence suggests that the R22 curriculum should be **modernized rather than completely replaced**. Fundamental concepts should be retained, while newer areas such as Generative AI, AI security, modern DevOps, cloud-native deployment, vector databases and post-quantum cryptography should be incorporated into appropriate existing courses or introduced as focused new components.

**Access date for sources: September 2026.**
