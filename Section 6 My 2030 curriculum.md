# 6. My 2030 Curriculum

## 6.1 Introduction

The proposed 2030 curriculum aims to preserve the fundamental knowledge of Computer Science while improving practical skills in modern software development, Artificial Intelligence, cloud computing, cybersecurity, and industry-oriented tools.

The redesign follows these principles:

* Maintain strong foundations in programming, data structures, algorithms, mathematics, operating systems, databases, and computer networks.
* Introduce modern AI and software engineering practices.
* Increase practical learning and project-based education.
* Maintain a total of **160 credits**, with approximately **20 credits in each semester**.
* Keep all additions within the assignment's approximate **20–25% redesign guideline (32–40 credits)**.
* Ensure that every major change is balanced through reductions, repurposing, or restructuring of existing curriculum components.

## 6.4 Prerequisite Structure

The proposed curriculum follows the following prerequisite relationships:

1. **Programming → Data Structures → Algorithms**

   Programming fundamentals are required before learning data structures and algorithm design.

2. **Statistics → Machine Learning → Deep Learning**

   Probability, statistics, and mathematical foundations support machine learning. Machine learning knowledge supports deep learning.

3. **Programming + DBMS + Software Engineering → Full Stack Development**

   Full Stack Development requires programming, database management, software engineering, and web development foundations.

4. **Operating Systems + Computer Networks → Cloud and DevOps**

   Knowledge of operating systems and networks is required for understanding containers, deployment, infrastructure, and distributed systems.

5. **AI + Machine Learning + Programming → Generative AI and LLM Systems**

   The student should understand basic AI and machine learning concepts before studying advanced AI application development.

6. **Machine Learning + Deep Learning → AI Deployment and MLOps**

   Model deployment and monitoring require prior knowledge of machine learning models and their evaluation.

7. **Computer Networks + Operating Systems + Cryptography → Cyber Security**

   Cybersecurity concepts are supported by networking, operating systems, and cryptography foundations.

## 6.5 Comparison with JNTUH R25

The comparison is made with the **JNTUH R25 curriculum for constituent engineering colleges**, which is relevant to JNTUH University College of Engineering Sultanpur. The affiliated-college version of R25 is not used for this comparison.

Important differences include:

* R25 moves Machine Learning to the second year, while this proposal retains it in the third year to provide more time for mathematical and statistical preparation.
* R25 introduces Deep Learning as a core subject with a laboratory component. This proposal also gives Deep Learning a structured position with practical learning.
* R25 introduces Prompt Engineering as a one-credit practical course. This proposal incorporates prompt engineering into a broader Generative AI and LLM Systems course instead of treating prompting as an isolated skill.
* R25 introduces Generative AI as a professional elective option. This proposal provides broader coverage of GenAI systems, including RAG, embeddings, evaluation, and AI agents.
* R25 promotes NLP and Cyber Security into core subjects. This proposal introduces Cyber Security as a core subject with a laboratory and incorporates NLP-related concepts into the GenAI and LLM Systems course.
* R25 reduces the constituent-college DevOps structure from 3 theory credits and 1 lab credit to a one-credit skill development component. This proposal retains four total DevOps credits but changes the distribution to 2 theory credits and 2 practical credits because practical experience is important for DevOps roles.

## 6.6 Why This Curriculum Is Different

The proposed curriculum differs from R25 in the following ways:

* It gives greater importance to practical learning and laboratory work.
* It treats Generative AI as a complete systems subject rather than only prompt engineering.
* It preserves the four-credit DevOps component while increasing practical exposure.
* It introduces AI deployment and MLOps to connect machine learning with production systems.
* It retains professional and open electives to allow students to explore different areas.
* It maintains strong foundational subjects instead of replacing them entirely with short-term technologies.
* It provides earlier exposure to AI and computing tools without moving advanced Machine Learning before its supporting prerequisites.

The objective is not to remove fundamental Computer Science knowledge, but to connect it with modern tools, practical development, and industry requirements.

## 6.7 Overall Credit and Redesign Justification

The redesigned curriculum retains the total of **160 credits**, with approximately **20 credits per semester**. Credit reductions are obtained from areas such as Engineering Graphics, Chemistry, Organizational Behavior, and selected project components. These credits are reallocated toward practical laboratories, AI literacy, Full Stack Development, Generative AI, Deep Learning, Cyber Security, and AI Deployment.

The redesign is intended to remain within the assignment's approximate 20–25% curriculum-change guideline. The change count includes reduced components, expanded practical components, and same-credit restructuring. The exact percentage depends on whether a same-credit change is counted as a content change or a credit change.

The central design principle is:

> Preserve foundational Computer Science subjects while updating the practical content, modernizing tools, and introducing important AI, security, cloud, and deployment capabilities.

### Redesign Percentage

The proposed curriculum contains 160 credits. The approximate redesign target is calculated as follows:

- Total curriculum credits: 160
- Target redesign range: 20–25%
- Equivalent redesign range: 32–40 credits

The proposed changes focus on restructuring existing subjects, improving practical components, and introducing industry-oriented learning. The exact redesigned credit count requires a detailed subject-by-subject comparison to avoid counting overlapping changes more than once.

Therefore, the redesign is planned to remain within the approximate 20–25% guideline, subject to final verification.


## 6.8 AI-Assisted A/B Testing Framework

### Purpose

AI-assisted A/B testing was planned to compare responses generated using two different prompts for five important CSE subjects. The purpose is to examine whether AI provides consistent, relevant, and practical curriculum recommendations.

### Subject 1: DevOps

**Prompt A:** Explain the importance of DevOps theory in a B.Tech CSE curriculum.

**Prompt B:** Can practical DevOps laboratory sessions replace DevOps theory? Explain with examples.

**Expected comparison:** The comparison should examine whether the responses identify the importance of both theoretical concepts and practical skills, including CI/CD, version control, Docker, automation, and deployment.

**Verification:** The recommendations should be compared with industry job descriptions and current DevOps practices.

### Subject 2: Machine Learning

**Prompt A:** Why should Machine Learning be included as a core subject in CSE?

**Prompt B:** What practical skills should students develop after studying Machine Learning?

**Expected comparison:** The comparison should examine coverage of machine learning fundamentals, data preprocessing, model evaluation, and implementation using programming libraries.

**Verification:** The suggested skills should be compared with relevant job descriptions and curriculum requirements.

### Subject 3: Deep Learning

**Prompt A:** Why should Deep Learning be introduced as a core subject in a B.Tech CSE curriculum?

**Prompt B:** What practical components should be included in a Deep Learning course?

**Expected comparison:** The comparison should examine coverage of neural networks, CNNs, model training, evaluation, and practical implementation using frameworks such as PyTorch or TensorFlow.

**Verification:** The proposed topics should be compared with research developments and relevant industry requirements.

### Subject 4: Natural Language Processing

**Prompt A:** Explain the relevance of NLP for future CSE graduates.

**Prompt B:** Design a practical NLP learning plan for undergraduate CSE students.

**Expected comparison:** The comparison should examine coverage of text preprocessing, embeddings, language models, transformers, and real-world projects.

**Verification:** The recommendations should be compared with research papers and relevant job descriptions.

### Subject 5: Cyber Security

**Prompt A:** Why is Cyber Security important in a modern CSE curriculum?

**Prompt B:** What theoretical and practical topics should a Cyber Security course contain?

**Expected comparison:** The comparison should examine whether the responses cover networking, cryptography, secure coding, authentication, and security testing.

**Verification:** The recommendations should be compared with the official curriculum and cybersecurity-related job requirements.

### Overall Observation

The A/B testing framework helps identify differences between broad conceptual prompts and practical implementation-focused prompts. AI responses can support brainstorming and organization, but important claims must be verified using official curriculum documents, research papers, and industry sources.

**Limitation:** The complete A/B tests could not be conducted within the available time. Therefore, this section presents the testing framework rather than claiming that all five tests were completed.
