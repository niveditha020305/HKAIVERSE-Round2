## 7.3 Evidence That Would Disprove the Central Claim (7a)

The central claim could be challenged if evidence shows that the existing R22 curriculum already provides sufficient practical training in modern industry tools and workflows.

Evidence that could disprove the claim includes:

- Existing laboratory courses effectively covering CI/CD, Docker, cloud computing, and automation.
- Industry feedback confirming that the current curriculum meets practical skill requirements.
- Evidence that students gain sufficient practical experience through existing projects, internships, and electives.
- Research showing that adding Deep Learning and NLP as core subjects does not significantly improve industry readiness.

Such evidence would require me to reconsider whether major curriculum changes are necessary or whether improving existing practical activities would be sufficient.

## 7.4 One Belief That Changed During the Analysis (7b)

### Initial Belief

I initially believed that DevOps theory was not very important and that practical laboratory work alone would be sufficient to learn DevOps skills.

### What Changed My Mind

During the curriculum analysis, I understood that theory and practical implementation serve different purposes. Practical activities are necessary for applying concepts such as CI/CD, Docker, cloud computing, and automation. However, theoretical knowledge helps students understand how these tools work and why they are used in real deployment workflows.

### Updated Belief

I now believe that DevOps theory and practical laboratory work should be taught together. This combination can help students develop conceptual understanding, apply industry tools, and become better prepared for modern software development environments.

### 8.2 Key Discussion Points

**Question 1: What are the major limitations of the current CSE curriculum?**

**Faculty Response:**

The current curriculum provides students with strong theoretical foundations in computer science. However, students need more opportunities to apply theoretical concepts through practical projects, industry tools, internships, and real-world problem-solving activities.

**Question 2: Should subjects such as DevOps and AI be included in the curriculum?**

**Faculty Response:**

Modern technologies such as DevOps, Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, and Cloud Computing are becoming increasingly important. These subjects should be introduced in a structured manner without removing the fundamental computer science subjects. Students should understand both the underlying concepts and their practical applications.

**Question 3: How can students become better prepared for industry requirements?**

**Faculty Response:**

Students should work on practical projects, use version control systems, participate in team-based development, and gain experience with deployment and testing tools. Continuous assessment through projects and laboratory work can help students develop problem-solving and implementation skills.

**Question 4: What should be considered when redesigning the curriculum?**

**Faculty Response:**

The curriculum should maintain a balance between fundamental subjects and emerging technologies. New subjects should have appropriate prerequisites, practical components, and sufficient time for students to understand the concepts. The curriculum should also be reviewed periodically to keep up with changes in the industry.

### 8.3 Key Insights Obtained

The discussion highlighted the following points:

- Practical learning should complement theoretical computer science foundations.
- DevOps should include both conceptual learning and hands-on laboratory activities.
- AI-related subjects should be introduced with suitable prerequisites.
- Project-based learning can help students apply their knowledge to real-world problems.
- Curriculum changes should maintain a balance between fundamentals and emerging technologies.

### 8.4 Unexpected Learning

One unexpected insight was that introducing new technologies alone may not be sufficient to improve industry readiness. The effectiveness of a curriculum also depends on how subjects are taught, the availability of practical activities, project-based assessment, and the connection between theoretical concepts and real-world applications.

This helped me understand that curriculum redesign should focus not only on adding new subjects but also on improving practical implementation and learning outcomes.
