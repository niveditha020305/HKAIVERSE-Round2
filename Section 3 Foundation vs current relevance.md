**Label Definitions**

* **KEEP:** The subject remains fundamental and relevant with little or no major change required.
* **UPDATE:** The subject remains important, but its topics, tools, examples or practical components need modernization.
* **REDUCE:** The subject has value, but its curriculum space should be reduced relative to more important CSE areas.
* **ADD:** A missing or insufficiently covered modern area that should be introduced into the curriculum.




### Evidence-Based Justification of Selected Labels

The following 12 labels were selected because the R22 syllabus provides specific evidence through its units, textbooks, tools and credit structure. The recommendations distinguish between subjects whose foundations should remain and subjects whose content or curriculum weight should change.

#### 1. CS503PC + CS505PC – DevOps + DevOps Lab — UPDATE

The R22 curriculum allocates 3 credits to DevOps theory and 1 credit to its laboratory, giving a total of 4 credits. The subject covers important concepts such as the DevOps lifecycle, source-code management, Git servers, Docker, Jenkins, automated testing and deployment.

However, some prescribed tools and practices require modernization. The curriculum should provide stronger hands-on experience with modern CI/CD platforms, container orchestration, infrastructure automation and cloud deployment.

Therefore, the subject should be retained with its total 4 credits but its credit distribution should be updated. The proposed redesign changes the structure from 3 theory credits and 1 laboratory credit to 2 theory credits and 2 practical credits.

The additional practical emphasis will allow students to gain more experience with GitHub Actions or GitLab CI, Docker, Kubernetes, Terraform, automated testing and cloud deployment. This approach maintains the importance of DevOps while improving practical and industry-oriented learning.

#### 2. CS601PC – Machine Learning — UPDATE

The prescribed textbook is Stephen Marsland's *Machine Learning: An Algorithmic Perspective*, 2nd edition (2014). The syllabus covers perceptrons, multilayer perceptrons, SVMs, decision trees, ensembles, dimensionality reduction, genetic algorithms, HMMs and Bayesian networks. These are useful foundations, but the syllabus does not cover modern architectures such as CNNs, attention mechanisms or Transformers.

Therefore, the subject should remain a core course but be updated with modern neural architectures, model deployment, practical ML pipelines and current applications.

The R22 placement of ML in III Year II Semester can also be defended. CS303PC Statistics is taught earlier and includes Bayes' Rule, probability distributions, hypothesis testing, Markov chains and stochastic processes. These topics directly support the Bayesian networks, HMMs and related concepts appearing in ML. Therefore, **the content should be updated while retaining the R22 placement** rather than blindly moving it earlier.

#### 3. CS603PC – Artificial Intelligence — UPDATE

The R22 syllabus uses Russell and Norvig's *Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach*, 3rd edition (2010). Its five units focus on classical AI including uninformed and informed search, adversarial search, propositional and first-order logic, planning and Bayesian reasoning.

These foundations remain valuable, so the course should not be replaced. However, the syllabus contains no formal coverage of modern AI systems such as large language models, attention mechanisms or retrieval-augmented generation.

The course should therefore be updated by retaining classical AI foundations while adding a modern AI unit covering concepts such as attention, LLMs, prompting, RAG and evaluation of generative AI systems.

#### 4. CS303PC – Computer Oriented Statistical Methods — UPDATE

The R22 syllabus covers important statistical foundations, including Bayes' Rule, probability distributions, sampling, hypothesis testing, Markov chains and stochastic processes. These topics support later subjects such as Machine Learning, Bayesian models and Hidden Markov Models.

However, the course can be improved by connecting theoretical concepts with practical computing and modern data analysis. The syllabus should include Python-based statistical programming, exploratory data analysis, statistical visualizations, model evaluation and practical applications related to machine learning.

Therefore, the subject should be updated rather than substantially reduced or removed. The core mathematical and statistical concepts should be retained, while practical programming and data-analysis activities should be added.

The proposed changes should preserve the prerequisite relationship between statistics and Machine Learning. Students should first develop the required probability and statistical foundations before applying them in advanced machine learning topics.
#### 5. CS404PC – Database Management Systems — UPDATE

The R22 DBMS syllabus focuses on relational database concepts including ER modelling, relational algebra, SQL, normalization, transactions, concurrency control and B+ tree indexing. These remain essential foundations.

However, the syllabus does not cover NoSQL/document databases or vector databases. Modern AI applications increasingly use embedding storage and vector search as part of retrieval-based systems.

Therefore, the relational foundation should be retained, but the syllabus should add NoSQL databases, cloud databases and vector databases such as PostgreSQL with pgvector. This would connect traditional database concepts with modern AI and application-development requirements.

#### 6. CS405PC – Software Engineering — UPDATE

The R22 syllabus covers software processes, requirements, design, UML, testing, metrics, risk management and quality management. These are still essential software-engineering foundations. However, the prescribed textbooks are Pressman's *Software Engineering: A Practitioner's Approach*, 6th edition, and Sommerville's *Software Engineering*, 7th edition. Unit I presents Waterfall, Spiral and Agile as major process models, while CI/CD is not integrated into the software-engineering course.

The subject should therefore be kept but updated to treat Agile development, CI/CD, automated testing, code review and modern software-development workflows as practical engineering practices. AI-assisted development and AI-generated testing can also be introduced as current software-engineering practices.

#### 7. CS308PC – Data Visualization: R Programming / Power BI — UPDATE

This course has a particularly clear curriculum-content mismatch. The course title specifies **R Programming / Power BI**, but the practical exercises and reference material use Tableau.

This creates a mismatch between what the course claims to teach and what the practical work actually develops. In addition, students later study Machine Learning where Python-based data workflows are highly relevant.

The course should therefore be updated by either aligning the practical work with the stated R/Power BI title or redesigning it around a consistent modern data-visualization workflow, including Python visualization and dashboard development. The course should also be given enough practical depth to connect with later data and ML courses.

#### 8. CS701PC – Cryptography and Network Security — UPDATE

The R22 course uses William Stallings' *Cryptography and Network Security*, 6th edition. Its coverage includes classical and widely used cryptographic concepts such as DES, AES, RSA, Diffie-Hellman, SHA-512, Kerberos, PKI, SSL/TLS and 802.11i.

These foundations remain important, but the syllabus does not include Post-Quantum Cryptography. NIST finalized its first three post-quantum cryptography standards — FIPS 203, FIPS 204 and FIPS 205 — in August 2024.

Therefore, the course should retain its cryptographic foundations but add modern security topics, especially post-quantum cryptography, modern TLS/security practices and contemporary application and API security.

#### 9. ME105ES – Computer Aided Engineering Graphics — REDUCE

ME105ES receives 3 credits in the first semester and covers engineering graphics topics such as conic sections, orthographic projections, isometric projections and solid development. It also includes computer-aided drafting and conventional drawing.

For a CSE degree, this has comparatively limited downstream relevance to the core computing curriculum. The first semester already contains programming and introductory CSE courses that are prerequisites for later technical subjects.

Therefore, the course should not necessarily be removed completely, but its credit weight can be reduced. The released credits can be redirected towards computing subjects that have stronger relevance to software and computing careers.

#### 10. CH102BS + CH107BS – Engineering Chemistry + Laboratory — REDUCE

Engineering Chemistry and its laboratory together account for 5 credits in the first year. The syllabus contains legitimate engineering topics such as water treatment, batteries, corrosion and polymers, but these have limited direct connection to the later CSE curriculum.

Therefore, the subject can be compressed into a smaller first-year component, for example by reducing the separate laboratory requirement or integrating practical activities into a shorter course.

This would free curriculum space without reducing the core CSE foundation, allowing the credits to be redirected towards modern computing areas.

#### 11. ADD – Deep Learning as a Core Subject — ADD

In R22, Deep Learning appears only as **CS863PE**, a Professional Elective VI option in the fourth year. This means a student can graduate from CSE without formally studying Deep Learning.

This is a significant gap because Deep Learning is now an important foundation for computer vision, NLP, generative AI and other AI applications.

The newer JNTUH R25 constituent-colleges curriculum addresses this by making Deep Learning a core subject with a laboratory. However, the redesign should not simply copy R25. The appropriate credit size and semester should be evaluated against the rest of the curriculum and the prerequisite relationship with Machine Learning.

A reasonable redesign is to introduce Deep Learning after Machine Learning, covering neural networks, CNNs, sequence models, attention/Transformers and practical model development.

#### 12. ADD – Generative AI / Prompt Engineering — ADD

R22 does not contain a dedicated Generative AI or Prompt Engineering course. R25 introduces a 1-credit Prompt Engineering practical and provides Generative AI as a Professional Elective II option.

For a 2030-oriented curriculum, this exposure should be strengthened. Prompt Engineering alone is not sufficient because modern AI application development also requires understanding RAG, embeddings, evaluation, model APIs and basic fine-tuning concepts.

Therefore, a small practical component for prompt engineering can be retained, but a broader **Generative AI Systems** course should be introduced as a core component. It should cover LLM fundamentals, prompt design, embeddings, RAG, evaluation, AI application development and responsible AI.
